# Task: scene references (refs.json) for my video editor

Use the FINAL narration script - the one you just wrote in this chat, or the one I attached / pasted below. It is the
exact text that will be recorded.

## What happens to your answer
My editing tool takes every "quote" you give and searches for it in the REAL subtitles of my footage. A match tells it
the exact second of that scene, and it cuts the picture from there. Then it uses "what" and "characters" to pick the
best shots around that moment. So:
- a quote that was really spoken = the right scene on screen,
- a wrong episode or an invented / paraphrased quote = wrong footage (or none),
- precision matters more than coverage. An empty quote is honest; a made-up one is harmful.

## Step 1 - go through the script sentence by sentence, in order
Give a ref for every sentence that points to something the viewer can SEE in the show/film:
- a concrete scene, event, action, decision, fight, conversation, reveal, death, arrest, speech;
- a sentence that quotes or paraphrases a character's line;
- a sentence about what a character did / said / looked like in a particular episode or film.
Skip sentences that are pure opinion, analysis, real-world facts, channel intro/outro, calls to action, or anything
not in the footage (actors, behind the scenes, ratings, real news) - unless the sentence clearly describes one
specific scene.
- A sentence that continues the same scene gets its own ref too (same episode; preferably a DIFFERENT line from that
  same scene, so a long scene gets quotes from its beginning, middle and end).
- A sentence that mentions two different moments gets two refs (same "line" text).
- General sentences about a character ("Walt changes over the seasons") may get a ref to the single most fitting
  scene if one clearly fits; otherwise skip.

## Step 2 - fields of each ref
- "line": the first 10-12 words of that script sentence, copied EXACTLY as written in the script (same language and
  alphabet, same spelling; do not translate, fix or shorten). If the sentence is shorter, copy the whole sentence.
- "show": the official title of the series or film, e.g. "The Wire", "Reacher", "Breaking Bad",
  "Jack Reacher: Never Go Back (2016)". For a film always add the year.
- "ep": for a series the episode code SxxEyy, e.g. "S04E09" (official season/episode order - check two-part episodes,
  specials and finales). For a film: the film title with year, e.g. "Jack Reacher (2012)".
- "quote": one line of dialogue REALLY SPOKEN ON SCREEN in that scene, word for word, in the language of the
  ORIGINAL footage (an English show discussed in a Hindi script still gets the English line). 4-20 words.
  THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT FIELD. Measured on my tool: with a real quote the exact moment is found almost every
  time; with keywords only, about 1 ref in 4 lands on the wrong scene. So aim for a real quote on at least 8 of every
  10 refs:
    1. for each episode/film the script uses, web-search its transcript ("<show> <episode title> transcript" - e.g.
       subslikescript, springfieldspringfield, tvshowtranscripts, fan-wiki quote pages, subtitle sites) and OPEN it;
    2. find the scene in the transcript and copy a line from it exactly - any line spoken in that moment works, it
       does not have to be the famous one;
    3. only if no transcript can be found, give the line as you remember it and mark "medium".
  NEVER invent, paraphrase or "improve" dialogue; if you truly have nothing, leave "quote" as "" and mark "low".
  A short real line beats a long approximate one. Do not reuse one line for different scenes.
- "keywords": 3-6 RARE words SPOKEN in that exact scene (objects, places, unusual nouns, guest characters' names), in
  the original footage language. Do NOT use the main characters' names or common words - they occur in every episode
  and send the search to the wrong scene. Backup search only; it never replaces the quote.
- "what": one plain sentence describing what is on screen, as if the sound were off: who is there, what they wear or
  hold, the place, day or night. Example: "Walt and Jesse argue inside the RV in the desert, daytime". Not ideas or
  judgements ("Walt's moral fall" is useless).
- "characters": the characters visible in that moment, full names where known, e.g. ["Walter White", "Jesse Pinkman"].
- "confidence": "high" = episode AND quote verified from a transcript / subtitle source; "medium" = episode certain,
  quote from memory; "low" = unsure.
