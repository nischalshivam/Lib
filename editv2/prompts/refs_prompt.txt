# Task: scene references (refs.json) for my video editor

Use ONE script only: the final version of the script you wrote last in this chat, all its parts, from the first hook
sentence to the last sentence. If a script is attached to this message, use that one instead. Ignore every other script
and draft in this chat. Do the whole job without asking me questions.

## Why this matters
My editing tool searches for every "quote" word for word in the real English subtitles of my own copy of the footage. A
match gives the exact second of the scene, and the tool takes its shots from the 45 seconds around it. Without a match
it guesses from "keywords" and often lands on the wrong scene. So the quote is the key field. It must be really spoken,
whole and word for word, from the right moment and the right episode. The quotes are only search keys for my editor;
nothing is published.

## Method: work EPISODE BY EPISODE
1. List every episode and film the script uses: code, official episode title, and the sentences it covers. Check each
   code against the episode list (Wikipedia). Take care with two-parters, specials, season openers and finales.
2. For each episode get the full dialogue:
   - the subtitle files I attached come first (unzip them; search them with code);
   - otherwise web-search "<show> <SxxEyy> <episode title> transcript" (film: "<title> <year> transcript") and OPEN the
     full page (subslikescript, springfieldspringfield, tvshowtranscripts, 8flix, a subtitle page). Never quote from
     search-result snippets, screenplays / shooting scripts, or quote and wiki pages.
   - Do not paste transcripts into the chat; only single lines go into the JSON.
3. With that dialogue open, write the refs for every sentence about that episode, then move to the next episode.
4. Output the refs in script order.

## Reply order
1. First reply: ONLY the episode table, then wait for "continue".
   - Line 1: Script used: "<its first 6 words> ... <its last 6 words>", <N> sentences, about <R> refs, <P> parts.
   - Then one row per episode or film: code - official title - transcript source (attached file / URL / NONE) -
     number of sentences it covers.
2. Each "continue": the next part (see Output). 3. After the last part: the summary.

## Which sentences get a ref
- Every sentence about something the viewer can SEE: a scene, action, event, conversation, reveal, or a line a
  character says. The opening hook is not an intro: every scene it describes gets a ref.
- Consecutive sentences about one scene: one ref each, same episode. Give each the line spoken at the point that
  sentence describes, so the quotes move FORWARD through the scene. If the scene has fewer good lines than sentences,
  leave the extra quotes empty (keep their keywords). Never use a line a third time, rotate lines, or borrow a line from
  another moment.
- A sentence that mentions two different moments: two refs with the same "line".
- A sentence that quotes or paraphrases a character: ONE ref, and its quote is that exact line.
- A sentence that sums up many scenes gets a ref only if one scene clearly shows it.
- No ref for opinion, analysis, real-world facts, channel intro/outro (welcome, subscribe, comment), calls to action,
  book-only or behind-the-scenes content. Never add refs to non-scene sentences to look complete - but never skip a
  scene sentence because its quote is hard to find: give the ref with an empty quote.

## Fields (each ref has exactly these 8 keys)
- "line": the first 10-12 words of the script sentence, copied EXACTLY (same language, alphabet and spelling, only
  without quotation marks). Never translate or fix it; never a sentence number. Shorter sentence: copy it whole. Fewer
  than 5 words: continue into the next sentence, up to 10 words.
- "show": the title the scene is really from, as on English Wikipedia, in Latin letters, even in a Hindi or Spanish
  script ("The Godfather (1972)", never "El Padrino"). Films always get their year. A scene from a spin-off, another
  series or a film gets THAT title, never the main show's.
- "ep": series: one "SxxEyy" code, the episode where the moment happens. Film: the film title with its year. Never
  "S01E01-E02", "Pilot", "Finale" or "S01".
- "quote": the dialogue spoken at the exact moment the sentence describes.
  - Ranking: a whole real line > an empty quote > anything else. If you cannot give the whole line word for word,
    leave it empty and mark "low" - except when you clearly remember the whole line in THAT scene: give it, mark
    "medium". A cut, shortened, reworded or composed line is worse than an empty one.
  - Always English, exactly as the English subtitles show it - also for Hindi / Spanish scripts and for scenes spoken in
    another language (give the English subtitle line). Copy it character for character, keeping spelling,
    contractions and numbers.
  - One speaker: one whole sentence, or two that this speaker says with nobody in between. 6-15 words, at least 3 words
    of 4+ letters. Only if the fitting sentence is longer than 15 words: copy its first 12-15 words, starting at its
    first word (the only allowed cut). Never start mid-sentence, never drop or change a word, never add "...".
  - Too short ("I need a name.")? Add that speaker's words right before or after it, word for word; else take the
    nearest longer line from that moment.
  - Spoken within about 30 seconds of what the sentence describes. A silent moment (a fight, a look, a body found): the
    nearest line inside those 30 seconds, or empty.
  - Distinctive: said only once in the episode, best with a name, place or unusual word - not "Let's go." or "I didn't
    kill anybody." Among equally good lines prefer one without digits, dotted acronyms or slang contractions.
  - Only from the episode where the scene happens: never from a "Previously on" recap (the first 1-2 minutes) or a
    preview; never on-screen text, signs or song lyrics.
  - Each quote at most twice, and only on adjacent sentences about the same moment.
  - No speaker names (WALT:), [sound tags], music notes, <i> tags or leading dashes.
  - When rules compete: right moment > whole real line > 6-15 words > distinctive.
- "keywords": a list of 3-5 single words spoken in that moment (or an unusual object clearly on screen), spelled as in
  the subtitles: guest-character names, places, objects, unusual nouns. NO main-cast names, NO common words (look,
  job, money, city, body, killed, time, trust). A fresh set for every scene. 3 sure words beat 5 doubtful ones.
- "what": English, max 20 words: what is on screen with the sound off - who, where, day or night when known, one
  visible action or object. No interpretation ("Walt's moral fall" is useless).
- "characters": a list of up to 4 people clearly in that moment, most important first, full English names, e.g.
  ["Walter White", "Jesse Pinkman"]; an unnamed person by role ("bartender"). Never empty.
- "confidence": "high" = quote copied from a subtitle file or transcript you opened; "medium" = whole quote from clear
  memory of that scene; "low" = no quote.

- Every "high" quote is 6-15 words, a whole line, English, from the named episode (not a recap), within ~30 s.
- Every quote used at most twice; every scene sentence has a ref (hook included); no opinion sentence has one.
- Refs in script order; every code matches its episode title; every "show" is the English title.
- No main-cast names or common words in keywords; no keyword set copied from another scene.
- Never break a rule to pass a check: no invented, cut or reused lines to fill quotes, no dropped refs.

## Output

  reply; I say "continue" for the next. Never shorten: no "...", "same as above" or placeholder refs.
- Valid JSON only: double quotes, no comments, no trailing commas. Each ref has exactly the 8 keys; no sources, links or
  notes inside the JSON. "keywords" and "characters" are lists of strings. Inside values: no line breaks, no "
  character (delete quotation marks from dialogue and script text), only the straight apostrophe '.
- Shape (format example only):

 {"line": "Walt finally tells Skyler who he really is, and it", "show": "Breaking Bad", "ep": "S04E06",
  "quote": "I am not in danger, Skyler. I am the danger.", "keywords": ["NASDAQ", "knocks", "belly"],

  "confidence": "high"}
